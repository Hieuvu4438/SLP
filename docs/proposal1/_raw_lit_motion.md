# Raw literature survey: Human Motion Generation techniques for transfer to Sign Language Production

Survey date: 2026-11-03. Scope: text-to-motion (T2M), whole-body / hands / face generation,
alignment objectives, evaluation critiques, and their transfer to 3D SMPL-X Sign Language
Production (SLP).

**How to read this file**
- Numbers are copied from the paper itself, or from a comparison table in a later paper that
  reproduces them. The source is named wherever it is not the paper itself.
- `[unverified]` means I could not confirm the item (venue, arXiv ID or number) against a
  primary source during this survey. It comes from memory or from a secondary source.
- HumanML3D numbers are on the **original Guo et al. (2022) 263-dim evaluator** unless a
  different evaluator is stated. Rows using different evaluators **cannot be compared** (see Section 5).
- Metric directions: FID lower is better; R-Precision (Top-1/Top-3) higher is better;
  MM-Dist lower is better. Real-motion reference on the original evaluator:
  Top-1 0.511, Top-3 0.797, FID 0.002, MM-Dist 2.974.

### 1.3 2025–2026 discrete successors and tokenizer research

- **MoMask++ / SnapMoGen** (2507.09122, NeurIPS 2025). Multi-scale RVQ with a shared codebook. Texts
  average about 48 words (HumanML3D about 12). An LLM rewrites casual prompts into the expressive
  style. Under the MARDM-67 evaluator (MoLingo's Table 1): FID 0.108, Top-1 0.492, Top-3 0.782.
- **Spatial-Temporal Multi-Scale Quantization (STMS/MSQ)** (2508.08991). Multi-scale quantization.
  It also reports a gap between R-Precision and a TMR-based success-rate metric.
- **FSQ / LFQ for motion.** ScaMo (FSQ), Being-M0 (2D-LFQ), and M3T for sign language (FSQ, see Section 6).
  I found **no controlled head-to-head study** of VQ vs FSQ vs LFQ on HumanML3D; the evidence is
  self-reported. M3T has the cleanest data point for our domain: face-codebook utilization goes
  from 78.8% (VQ) to 99.0% (FSQ).
- **Hierarchical and part-wise tokenizers.** ParCo (6 parts), HumanTOMATO H²VQ (body + hand
  codebooks), MotionGPT-2 Part-Aware VQ-VAE, MoGenTS (per joint), and EMAGE (4 compositional VQ-VAEs
  for face, upper body, hands and lower body).
- **DiMo** (2602.04188; discrete diffusion for generation and understanding) and **MotionGPT3**
  (2603.26747; moves from diffusion to flow) [both seen only in search results; details unverified].

## 4. Training objectives and alignment

### 4.1 Contrastive text-motion models

- **TMR** (2305.00976, ICCV 2023 [venue from memory]). A TEMOS-style VAE with contrastive InfoNCE
  and filtering of negatives with similar texts. It became the backbone for alignment losses
  (HumanTOMATO), evaluators (MotionStreamer's 272-dim evaluator, TMR-R-Precision) and DPO labelling
  (MoDiPO).
- **LaMP** (ICLR 2025). Contrastive, matching, captioning and generation pretraining gives a text
  encoder that improves T2M on all metrics. It shows that **replacing CLIP text features with
  motion-aligned text features is a large, cheap gain**.
- **MoLingo** (CVPR 2026). Aligning the *motion latent* to frame-level text makes the diffusion
  space easier to model.

### 4.3 Other objectives and inference tricks

- **Classifier-free guidance (CFG).** Standard in MDM, MLD and MoMask (logit-space CFG in masked
  decoding). ReMoDiffuse adds condition-mixture CFG over text and retrieval conditions. CFG scale
  strongly trades FID against R-Precision. The Top-1 > real-motion results above are partly a CFG
  effect [interpretation].
- **Physics and contact losses.** MDM foot-contact and velocity losses, ReinDiffuse physics
  rewards, and HY-Motion physics reward.
- **Length prediction.** MoMask and MMM need a length estimator (from T2M). BAMM removes it by
  predicting the end token implicitly. Diffusion models need the length given.
- **LLM planning and rewriting.** Motion-Agent (GPT-4 planner), SnapMoGen (LLM rewrites casual
  prompts into expressive descriptions), and the hierarchical text descriptions in Being-M0.
- **Retrieval augmentation.** ReMoDiffuse for generic motion; SOKE (dictionary signs) and SignRR in
  SLP (Section 6).
- **Representation choices.** MARDM shows redundant features hurt diffusion; ACMDM shows absolute
  coordinates make control easy; both use v-prediction and latent AEs.

## 5. Evaluation critiques for text-to-motion

1. **The evaluator weights redundant dimensions (MARDM, CVPR 2025).** The Guo et al. evaluator is
   trained on all 263 dims. Perturbing only redundant dims raises FID from 2.021 to 21.032, which
   systematically penalizes diffusion models (per-dim continuous errors). The paper provides
   MARDM-67 (GRU) and CLIP-style evaluators. MoLingo (CVPR 2026) reports MARDM-67, MS-272 and TMR-263.
2. **R-Precision saturation and resemblance to the training set.** DiMo (2602.04188) notes that real
   motion scores Top-1 0.511, below several generators, and that "higher R-Precision may largely
   reflect that the generated sequences resemble training motions". SALAD (0.581), LaMP (0.557) and
   Motion Anything (0.546) all exceed real motion.
3. **Outdated, weak evaluator.** RoleMotion (2512.01582) and Progressive Motion Generation
   (2503.13300) say the T2M evaluator cannot separate real from modern generated motion, and they
   train new ones.
4. **Metric disagreement.** STMS (2508.08991) finds R-Precision disagrees with TMR-based success rates.
5. **Representation-specific evaluators.** MotionStreamer's TMR-based evaluator on 272-dim gives
   FIDs of about 12, which are not comparable with the about-0.05 FIDs on Guo-263.
6. **Human-perception gap.** MotionCritic (ICLR 2025) motivates learned perceptual critics, and AToM
   shows VLM judges are only about 70% human-consistent.
7. **Single-caption assumption.** R-Precision and MM-Dist treat one caption as the only correct text
   (secondary source).

**SLP analogue.** "Meaningful Pose-Based Sign Language Evaluation" (2510.07453) meta-evaluated
keypoint-distance, embedding (SignCLIP) and back-translation (BT) metrics. **BT, especially the
likelihood score, was the most consistent**. SignCLIP worked well for sign-level retrieval but was
weaker for sentences and for unseen languages. SignVQNet (2309.12179) found DTW-MJE inconsistent.
BackTranslation2.0 [arXiv ID unverified] proposes an LLM-assisted BT metric for BSL.
MaDiS (2601.19577) introduces SiBLEU and SiCLIP.

## 7. Transferable techniques for Sign Language Production

SLP-specific requirements:
- **R1** finger articulation precision (handshape is phonemic);
- **R2** temporal alignment to gloss / words and co-articulation;
- **R3** non-manual features (NMF: mouthing, brows, head);
- **R4** back-translation (BT) accuracy, which is what Deaf-relevant evaluation best tracks (2510.07453);
- **R5** small data (10^2–10^3 h vs 10^3–10^4 h for general motion).

### 7.1 Ranked by expected gain

| Rank | Technique (source) | Targets | Expected gain | Already in SLP? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Back-translation / semantic reward alignment.** DPO / SoPo / GRPO with a frozen sign-to-text (SLT) model as the reward, plus a physics / hand-plausibility reward (MoDiPO, SoPo, MotionRL multi-reward, ReinDiffuse, HY-Motion RL, AToM) | R4, R1 | **High.** BT is the metric that tracks quality best, and it can be optimized directly. Multi-reward (BT + FID-proxy + jerk / penetration) avoids reward hacking | **No for production.** DPO / GRPO exist only for SLT (SignDPO 2604.18034, SA-GRPO 2608.00235, RVLF 2512.07273). M3T uses BT only as an auxiliary supervised loss |
| 2 | **Motion-aligned text encoder / semantic latent** (LaMP, MoLingo, SeRV-style token-level text supervision, HumanTOMATO TMR loss) | R4, R2 | **High.** LaMP's gain over CLIP-conditioned models comes mostly from the text side. A text–SMPL-X-sign contrastive model (a "Sign-TMR") would also give a reusable evaluator and a reward | Partial: SeRV (semantic RVQ), MaDiS (tri-level pretraining). **No public SMPL-X Sign-TMR-style evaluator** with R-Precision / FID |
| 3 | **Part-wise / hierarchical tokenizers with FSQ or RVQ** (H²VQ, ParCo, MoGenTS per-joint, EMAGE compositional, ScaMo FSQ, MoMask RVQ, MoMask++ multi-scale) | R1, R3 | **High for hands and face.** Per-hand codebooks with RVQ or FSQ avoid collapse on low-variance face and finger channels (M3T: 78.8%→99.0% utilization) | Yes, partially (SOKE, M3T, SignRR, SeRV, MaDiS). **Per-joint 2D token maps (MoGenTS) and multi-scale shared-codebook RVQ (MoMask++) not found in SLP** |

### 7.2 Reasoning per requirement

- **R1 Hands.** HumanTOMATO and EMAGE show that dedicated hand codebooks are necessary: a shared
  body codebook under-represents fingers. In MoMask, RVQ depth trades reconstruction against
  predictability; SeRV adds text-aligned residuals to keep the deeper layers predictable. The
  quantization ceiling remains, so continuous refinement is attractive: DisCoRD (rectified-flow
  decoding of tokens) or a MAR-style continuous head. A cheap, testable add-on is a contact-aware
  guidance term on fingertip-to-face / fingertip-to-hand distances.
- **R2 Temporal alignment.** Masked models need a length; BAMM-style implicit EOS or a gloss-duration
  regulator (GLOS, Text2Sign Diffusion) fits. LaMP / MoLingo frame-level text alignment and
  multi-token cross-attention directly encourage word-to-segment correspondence. Hierarchical flow
  (MotionHiFlow) separates coarse timing from fine articulation.
- **R3 NMF.** Face channels have low variance and collapse under VQ (M3T); FSQ plus a FLAME
  expression space (BEAT2 / SMPL-FX) is the current best recipe. EMAGE's "body hints → face"
  conditioning is a transferable design for mouthing synchronized to manual signs.
- **R4 BT.** The strongest lever, because both the motion literature (AToM, MotionRL, SoPo) and
  the SLP evaluation literature (2510.07453) point to it. Use a frozen SLT model (trained on real
  SMPL-X sign) as the reward. Keep a *separate* held-out BT model for evaluation to avoid
  Goodhart effects. This is not yet done for production in the literature I found.
- **R5 Data.** ReMoDiffuse / SOKE / SignRR retrieval and general-motion pretraining are the two
  levers. The ScaMo / Being-M0 scaling evidence says vocabulary size should scale with data, so
  sign tokenizers should use a smaller FSQ grid than general-motion ones [inference].

### 7.3 Gaps: techniques not found applied to SLP

Searched with "sign language" + technique; absence is "as far as found", not proof.

1. **DPO / RLHF / GRPO for sign *production*** with BT, critic or physics rewards. Only SLT has these
   (SignDPO, SA-GRPO, RVLF, SignRAG).
2. **A TMR-style text–3D-sign contrastive evaluator** with R-Precision / FID / MM-Dist as a standard
   SMPL-X SLP protocol. SignCLIP is video-based; MaDiS proposes SiCLIP but it is not established.
3. **MAR-style masked autoregression with a per-token continuous diffusion head** (MARDM,
   MotionStreamer) on SMPL-X sign latents.
4. **Per-joint 2D token maps** (MoGenTS) and **multi-scale shared-codebook RVQ** (MoMask++) for hands.
5. **Consistency distillation** (MotionLCM) for SLP.
6. **Hand-contact / penetration guidance** (OmniControl / HOI style) for sign contact phonemes.
7. **Pretraining on large general whole-body motion** (Motion-X++, MotionMillion) before SLP fine-tuning.
8. **A learned perceptual critic from Deaf-signer preferences** (MotionCritic analogue).
9. **Redundant-representation / evaluator critiques** (MARDM-style) applied to SMPL-X SLP features
   and to the back-translators used in SLP evaluation.
10. **Mamba / lightweight models** (Motion Mamba, Light-T2M) for SLP. No hits found.

### 7.4 Suggested first experiments (fit with this repo's SOKE baseline)

1. Add **BT-reward preference fine-tuning** (SoPo-style semi-online DPO) on top of SOKE tokens. The
   reward is a frozen SLT model; the evaluation BT model is a different, held-out one.
2. Replace SOKE's hand VQ with **RVQ or FSQ per hand**, and measure hand reconstruction (MPJPE on
   fingertips) and token utilization.
3. Build a **Sign-TMR** on CSL-Daily / How2Sign SMPL-X. Use it both as a LaMP-style text encoder for
   the generator and as an R-Precision / FID evaluator. Report it alongside BT, never instead of BT.
